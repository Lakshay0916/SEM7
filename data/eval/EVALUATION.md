# Agent evaluation

42 runs of the full self-healing loop (fresh commit, real tests, analysis, approval, repair,
real re-test, reflection, PR). Generated by `scripts/run_evaluation.py`; per-run data in `runs.jsonl`.
Medians with the interquartile range in brackets. Cost = tokens x Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite's list price
($0.25 / $1.5 per million input / output tokens). Total: 363 LLM calls,
583,406 tokens, $0.2320, 0.69 h of runs.

## Findings

- **Reliability:** the agents fixed 36 of 36 runs on their own, 36 on the first attempt, with the root cause naming the broken function in 36 of 36.
- **Against the rules baseline:** the rules also fixed every run, but on the first attempt only 50% of the time (scenario 3 needs a second attempt); the agents' price for one-attempt fixes is a median 12,603 tokens ($0.0058) per run.
- **Tokens by pattern:** parallel used 2.3x the tokens of sequential (median 25,723 vs 11,244) - three specialists plus a lead read the evidence instead of one investigator; hierarchical stayed close to sequential (12,385).
- **Time by pattern:** median analysis 31 s sequential, 24 s parallel, 78 s hierarchical. Sequential and parallel overlap (no clear difference); hierarchical is the slowest, as the lead's delegations add LLM calls one after another (median 11 vs 8), and bursts of calls meet the free tier's rate limit.
- **Blackboard vs direct:** median tokens 13,975 vs 12,385 and time 45 s vs 27 s - the ranges overlap, so no clear cost difference; the blackboard's gains are its record (every post saved), retries with feedback and decoupled agents, not cheaper runs.
- **Guardrail:** all 2 injected-commit runs were blocked before any LLM call (0 tokens) and repaired by the rules.
- **Long tails:** most analyses finish in under a minute, but the slowest took 816 s (agents-simple_bug-parallel-blackboard-3); its trace shows a single LLM call waiting for minutes inside the SDK on the free tier. Medians are therefore the fair comparison.

## How to read this

- "Fixed" = the loop reached a pull request; "fixed by agents alone" also requires that no attempt fell
  back to the rule-based engine (a fallback happens when Gemini errors or a plan fails the guardrail).
- Times were measured on Gemini's free tier (15 requests per minute). Rate-limit waits are part of the
  times and show up as LLM errors / retries; they inflate the patterns that make many calls in a burst.
- 36 agent runs across 12 configurations is enough to see large
  differences (e.g. tokens per pattern), not small ones: overlapping ranges mean "no clear difference".
